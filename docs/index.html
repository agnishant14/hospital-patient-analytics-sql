      Data provenance

      The patterns were planted. The job was to dig them back out.

      This data is synthetic, deliberately. The generator writes known rules into 50,000 rows, which turns every rule into a test with an answer known in advance. The warehouse and its twelve queries have to recover them — and a second implementation in Python, working from the same written spec and never touching SQL Server, recomputes every published figure independently. If the two disagree by a single paisa, the build fails.

      What the SQL recovered
      Emergency Medicine is given a 50-minute base wait. Every other department starts between 12 and 36.
      Emergency averages 67.27 min — fourteen minutes clear of the next worst department, and the only one that never sees a patient inside 30 minutes.
      ✓
      Satisfaction is a step function of wait: 25, 40, 55 and 70 minutes each cost a star.
      Every department's mean score lands exactly where its wait distribution puts it. Emergency sits at 1.75; Cardiology, 26 minutes quicker, at 3.46.

      Annual volume steps up on fixed boundaries: 5,000 to 6,000 to 7,500 to 8,500 to 10,500 to 12,500.

      486 patients are written in as one-time visitors; the other 1,945 are drawn from repeatedly.
      Repeat rate 80.01% — 1,945 of 2,431 patients, averaging 20.57 visits each.
